# FermentAI: four-slide pitch (3 minutes)

The rules for every slide: one idea, a headline of 8 words or fewer, no more than 3 bullets, and one
figure. Colours are the same everywhere: Chamber A is blue `#1f77b4` and Chamber B is orange `#ff7f0e`.
Provenance badges are green for REAL, purple for SIMULATED and grey for REPLAY/EXAMPLE. The figures live
in `data/figures/` and are regenerated by `uv run python scripts/make_pitch_figures.py`.

Timing assumes about 140 words per minute. The notes total 392 words, which is 2 min 48 s.

Placeholders that wait for the GPU numbers look like this: **[ALCHEMI: … — pending]**. Do not read a
placeholder aloud. Say the fallback sentence given next to it.

---

## Slide 1: hook

**Headline:** AI scientists that ferment, taste and iterate

**Figure:** `data/figures/architecture.png`

- Sourdough flavour is set by temperature over 12–16 h, and bakers tune it by feel.
- Two real chambers, each run by its own AI scientist agent.
- The whole demo runs on a local network. No internet needed.

**Speaker notes (~90 words, ~40 s)**

> Every sourdough baker runs an experiment every day, then throws the data away. Temperature over a
> twelve-to-sixteen-hour ferment decides how sour and how airy the loaf gets, and people tune it by feel.
> FermentAI turns a kitchen into an instrumented lab. There are two independent chambers. Each has an
> ESP32, a Peltier element that heats and cools, sensors and a camera, and its own AI scientist agent.
> The two agents share findings and argue over the local network. Everything you'll see runs on this Mac
> and a Wi-Fi access point, with no internet required.

## Slide 2: the closed loop

**Headline:** Your taste picks the next experiment

**Figure:** `data/figures/closed_loop.png` (backup or zoom-in: `data/figures/agents_exchange.png`)

- The judge types plain words, and the system turns them into structured sensory preferences.
- Agent A proposes a new temperature schedule, and Agent B critiques it.
- Chef-hats animate and the chambers run the new schedule. The LLM never controls power.

**Speaker notes (~95 words, ~41 s)**

> This is the core idea. You taste two loaves and type whatever you think on the iPad: too sour, a bit
> dense, anything. The system turns that into sensory preferences and compares them with our recorded
> runs and the simulation. Agent A proposes a new temperature schedule. Agent B checks it against its
> own model and pushes back: "that will overproof, cool it at six hours." The agreed experiment is
> published and the chef-hats wiggle. Safety is separate, because the API and the firmware clamp every
> setpoint. Offline, a labelled deterministic policy does the same job.

## Slide 3: the evidence pipeline

**Headline:** Real GPU chemistry, honest simulation, printable crumb

**Figure:** `data/figures/evidence_pipeline.png`

- NVIDIA ALCHEMI (AIMNet2) on a Brev GPU computes real lactic vs acetic acid energetics. **[ALCHEMI: ΔE_assoc lactic vs acetic — pending]**
- A kinetics model (simulated) predicts pH, acids, CO₂ and rise for each chamber.
- These map to 3D-printable crumb specimens, which are then checked against real loaf photos.

**Speaker notes (~100 words, ~43 s)**

> Every claim has a source, labelled real or simulated. Step one is real. NVIDIA ALCHEMI, running
> AIMNet2 on a Brev GPU, computes how lactic and acetic acid bind water and give up a proton, and the
> result is **[ALCHEMI: ΔE_assoc lactic vs acetic — pending]**. That chemistry only nudges the acid
> terms of a separate fermentation model. It does not predict bread. The model is simulated. It
> predicts pH, acids, gas and rise, which we map to crumb porosity and print as Formlabs specimens: 245
> small pores for the mild loaf, 84 large ones for the sour. Finally, we photograph the real crumb and
> compare.

*Until the GPU numbers are filled in, say "…give up a proton. Those energies are cached on this Mac."
in place of the placeholder clause. Never read the bracket aloud.*

## Slide 4: results, demo and what's next

**Headline:** Warmer ferments sourer, then the agents intervene

**Figure:** `data/figures/results_ab.png`

- Simulated: at 16 h, B reaches pH 3.90 vs 4.13 for A, with 6.5 vs 4.7 g/kg lactic acid.
- B overproofs from 9.5 h. Cooling it to 22 °C at 6 h delays that to about 11 h.
- Next: real bakes recalibrate the model, then more kitchens join the network.

**Speaker notes (~95 words, ~41 s)**

> Here's what the model predicts. It is simulated, and the badge says so. The warmer chamber B
> acidifies faster: pH 3.9 against 4.1 at sixteen hours, and about forty percent more lactic acid. It
> also peaks two hours earlier and starts to overproof at nine and a half hours. The agents catch that.
> Cooling B at six hours pushes overproofing out to about eleven. Next, real loaves and sensor data
> recalibrate the model, and the same loop scales to a network of kitchens sharing evidence. Let's run
> it. Please taste, and tell the iPad what you think.
