# FermentAI: four-slide investor pitch (3 minutes)

The audience is investors and engineers who have never heard of the project. The rules for every
slide: one idea, a headline of 8 words or fewer, no more than 3 bullets, plain language, and every
claim labelled **real**, **simulated** or **pending**, because investors read diligence.

Colours are the same everywhere: Chamber A is blue `#1f77b4` and Chamber B is orange `#ff7f0e`.
Badges are green for REAL, purple for SIMULATED / IN-SILICO and grey for REPLAY/EXAMPLE. Yellow boxes
mark pending numbers. The figures live in `data/figures/` and are regenerated by
`uv run python scripts/make_pitch_figures.py`. That script reads every number from the data files.
Partner names appear as plain-text wordmarks only. Don't add trademarked logos without permission.

Timing assumes about 140 words per minute. The spoken notes total 391 words, which is
2 min 48 s (see the table at the end). Bracketed placeholders are read as a short phrase, or skipped,
until they are filled.

## Slide 1: problem and vision

**Headline:** Every kitchen can become a food-science lab

**Figure:** `data/figures/closed_loop.png`

- **Problem:** fermented-food flavour is tuned by guesswork, and every bake's data is thrown away.
- **Vision:** decentralised AI scientists run experiments in real kitchens and share the evidence.
- **Why now:** GPU chemistry costs cents, sensors cost dollars, and AI agents can reason and argue.

**Speaker notes (94 words, 40 s)**

> Every sourdough baker runs an experiment every day, then throws the data away. Flavour depends on
> temperature over a twelve-to-sixteen-hour ferment, and people tune it by feel. Our vision is that
> every kitchen becomes a lab, with AI scientists that run experiments, share evidence and learn from
> the people who eat the result. Why now? Molecular chemistry on a GPU costs cents, sensors cost
> dollars, and AI agents can reason and argue. This loop is the product. You bake, you taste, you
> tell the system what you think, and it designs the next experiment.

## Slide 2: the pipeline, step by step

**Headline:** Seven steps from sensors to printed crumb

**Figure:** `data/figures/evidence_pipeline.png`. Put `data/figures/architecture.png` as an inset or
on the backup slide, and point at its "Demo needs no internet" banner.

- Sense the chambers, simulate fermentation, then add NVIDIA ALCHEMI molecular evidence.
- The agents debate, a person tastes and types feedback, and the next experiment is scheduled.
- Each chamber's prediction becomes a Formlabs-printed crumb hypothesis, checked against real loaf photos.

**Speaker notes (97 words, 42 s)**

> Here's the pipeline, step by step, and each step is labelled real or simulated. One: two chambers
> sense temperature, humidity, gas and rise. Two: a fermentation model simulates acidity and rise.
> Three: NVIDIA ALCHEMI adds real molecular evidence about the acids. Four: two AI agents debate what
> to try next. Five: a person tastes and types plain feedback. Six: the agreed experiment goes to
> the chambers, with safety limits enforced in firmware. Seven: each prediction becomes a printed
> crumb hypothesis, which we check against photos of the real loaf. All of it runs on a laptop,
> offline.

## Formlabs status (for diligence questions)

When this was written there was no `.progress/formlabs.md` and no `docs/formlabs_workflow.md`. The
repo's `src/fermentai/geometry/formlabs_preform.py` was a description-only plan with these steps:

1. Create a scene.
2. Import the STL.
3. Auto-orient, add supports and lay out.
4. Validate.
5. Save the `.form` file.
6. Print, but only by a human. This step is never automated.

The PreForm integration is therefore described as "prepared via the PreForm Local API". Print-time
and resin estimates are **pending**, and both `tech_highlights.png` and slide 3 show placeholders for
them. When the integration agent reports real estimates:

- set `PREFORM_ESTIMATES` in `scripts/make_pitch_figures.py`;
- re-run the script;
- replace the slide 3 placeholder.
