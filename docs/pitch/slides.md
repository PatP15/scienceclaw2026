# FermentAI: four-slide pitch (3 minutes)

The rules for every slide: one idea, a headline of 8 words or fewer, no more than 3 bullets, and one
main figure. Colours are the same everywhere: Chamber A is blue `#1f77b4` and Chamber B is orange
`#ff7f0e`. Provenance badges are green for REAL, purple for SIMULATED / IN-SILICO and grey for
REPLAY/EXAMPLE. The figures live in `data/figures/` and are regenerated by
`uv run python scripts/make_pitch_figures.py`, which reads every number from the data files.

Timing assumes about 140 words per minute. The spoken notes total 403 words, which is 2 min 53 s (see
the table at the end).

---

## Slide 1: hook

**Headline:** AI scientists that ferment, taste and iterate

**Figure:** `data/figures/architecture.png`

- Sourdough flavour is set by temperature over 12–16 h, and bakers tune it by feel.
- Two chambers, each run by its own AI scientist agent.
- The whole demo runs on a local network. No internet needed.

**Speaker notes (94 words, 40 s)**

> Every sourdough baker runs an experiment every day, then throws the data away. Temperature over a
> twelve-to-sixteen-hour ferment decides how sour and how airy the loaf gets, and people tune it by feel.
> FermentAI turns a kitchen into an instrumented lab. There are two independent chambers. Each has an
> ESP32, a Peltier element that heats and cools, sensors and a camera, and its own AI scientist agent.
> The two agents share findings and argue over the local network. Everything you'll see runs on this Mac
> and a Wi-Fi access point, with no internet required.

## Slide 2: the closed loop

**Headline:** Your taste picks the next experiment

**Figure:** `data/figures/closed_loop.png` (backup or zoom-in: `data/figures/agents_exchange.png`)

- The judge types plain words, and the system turns them into structured sensory preferences.
- Agent A proposes a new temperature schedule, and Agent B critiques it.
- Chef-hats animate and the chambers run the new schedule. The LLM never controls power.

**Speaker notes (101 words, 43 s)**

> This is the core idea. You taste two loaves and type whatever you think on the iPad: too sour, a bit
> dense, anything. The system turns that into sensory preferences and compares them with our recorded
> runs and the simulation. Agent A proposes a new temperature schedule. Agent B checks it against its
> own model and pushes back: "that will overproof, cool it at six hours." The agreed experiment is
> published and the chef-hats wiggle. Safety is separate, because the API and the firmware clamp every
> setpoint. With no internet, a deterministic Offline Policy runs the same loop, labelled on screen.

## Slide 3: the evidence pipeline

**Headline:** Real GPU chemistry, honest simulation, printable crumb

**Figure:** `data/figures/evidence_pipeline.png`

- **Real:** ALCHEMI ran AIMNet2 on a Brev GPU (159 optimisations, 72 MD runs, ~$0.68). In a three-water cluster, lactic acid ionises ~7.5 kcal/mol more easily than acetic acid.
- It constrains one acid term in the kinetics model (potency 0.10 → 0.124, pH changes by < 0.01). It does not drive the bread.
- **Simulated:** each chamber's kinetics produce one printable crumb specimen (A 39 % porosity / 92 pores, B 34 % / 53). These are then checked against real loaf photos.

**Speaker notes (107 words, 46 s)**

> Every claim has a source, labelled real or simulated. Step one is real. NVIDIA ALCHEMI ran AIMNet2
> on a Brev GPU: a hundred and fifty-nine optimisations and seventy-two dynamics runs, for about seventy
> cents. In small water clusters, lactic acid binds water slightly more strongly and gives up its
> proton about seven kilocalories more easily than acetic acid. That only constrains one acid term in a
> separate fermentation model. pH moves by less than a hundredth, so it doesn't drive the bread. The
> model is simulated. Its outputs map to crumb structure, printed as one Formlabs specimen per chamber.
> Finally, we photograph the real crumb and compare.

## Slide 4: results, the benchmark and the demo

**Headline:** Warmer ferments sourer, then the agents intervene

**Figure:** `data/figures/results_ab.png`. Put `data/figures/benchmark_summary.png` on the right third,
or use it as the backup slide.

- Simulated: at 16 h, B reaches pH 3.90 vs 4.13 for A, with 6.5 vs 4.7 g/kg lactic acid. B overproofs from 9.5 h.
- Cooling B to 22 °C at 6 h delays overproofing to about 11 h.
- In-silico benchmark: talking agents change their pick in 3.9 of 4 rounds and gain +0.68 nats. The final recommendation does not change.

**Speaker notes (101 words, 43 s)**

> Here's what the model predicts. It is simulated, and the badge says so. The warmer chamber B
> acidifies faster: pH 3.9 against 4.1 at sixteen hours, with about forty percent more lactic acid. It
> also overproofs from nine and a half hours, and cooling it at six hours pushes that to about eleven.
> Does talking help? In thirty simulated worlds, agents sharing a log change their choice almost every
> round and stop duplicating experiments. The information gain is modest, and the final recommendation
> stays the same. Next, real bakes recalibrate the model. Please taste, and tell the iPad what you
> think.
